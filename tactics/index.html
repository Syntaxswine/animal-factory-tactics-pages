    WORKERS’ FIELD MANUAL / 01

    Four comrades. One factory.

    Defeat the guards in each local map. Your squad is tougher and more accurate, but open ground is dangerous. Explore at your own pace; spotting an opponent starts turn-based combat immediately.

    Move, aim, act

    The cursor shows the available action: boot to walk, crosshair to target a guard, hand to select a teammate or interact, and bolt cutters to open a wire fence. With cutters held, click an adjacent wire fence to cut it. Click reachable supplies to open the inventory, or a nearby travel marker to open the overmap. Select a squad card or press 1–4. Shift-click cards or characters to toggle group membership; Select all on layer selects the conscious squad on that floor. The primary character fires and uses equipment. Group orders preserve relative destination spacing where possible and stop together on contact or insufficient AP. Click open ground once to preview movement, then click the same destination again to walk. Click any tile, including unexplored ground, to set a destination. Routes through unknown terrain are tentative: the character moves toward the point and replans when obstacles become visible. New enemy contact, exhausted AP or Escape stops movement. Hover to preview the estimated route and cost. During combat, standing movement costs 2 AP per tile, kneeling 4, and prone 8. Diagonal steps cost1.5×:3 AP standing,6 kneeling,12 prone. Both adjoining tiles and all four corner edges must be clear. Any change to a different stance costs 2 AP, including standing directly to prone. Stance changes are free outside combat. Stand before using stairs, ladders or roof climbs; stairs cost 2 AP per level and ladders cost 3. Marked roof edges (gold R arrows) cost 6 AP up or down and connect one level only. Stand on a cyan stair marker and use the climb ↑ or ↓ buttons. Movement stops on first contact. Use Escape to stop a route.

    Click an enemy once to select them, choose an aim location (torso by default), then click them again to fire after reviewing the hover odds. A changed combat situation requires selecting the target again. Escape cancels shot confirmation. You can also press Attack / F. End the squad turn with Space after using all four characters. Guards then move and fire. Clear the alerted guards to resume exploration.

    Campaign time

    The shared clock starts at Day 1, 08:00. One real second advances one game minute during active play, including combat and the overmap. The clock pauses while this tab is hidden or the field manual is open. On a cleared map, use Overmap to rest or train for 1, 4, 8, 24 or 48 hours. Ordinary rest heals gradually over about two days and refills AP. Rest with medical care requires a living troop with Medical 25 or higher and one squad medkit per wounded troop; it heals over about one day. A kit covers 24 hours of assisted rest, including later short rests, and is not consumed for healthy troops. Dead and captured troops cannot recover; training grants 25 XP per hour to each available troop below level 10. Training points are assigned on the character sheet. Travel takes one hour. Liberated factories produce income throughout all elapsed time. Restart or reload resets the current run and its clock.

    Training

    Every defeated guard awards 25 XP to surviving squad members. Every 100 XP grants a level and three assignable points, up to level 10. Train shooting, medical, stealth, vitality or mobility between encounters; no fixed classes; learned skills are permanent and there are no respecs. Each skill caps at 20 investments. Species modifiers are separate and displayed on the character sheet. Increasing maximum HP or AP does not heal or refill current resources.

    Stealth and reactions

    Sneaking adds 2 AP per cardinal tile and reduces detection range (60 − 20 − stealth × 0.2, minus another 10 prone; minimum 8). Movement noise reaches 10 tiles normally or 3 sneaking; gunfire reaches 30. Faded ? markers show remembered enemy positions, not current locations. Unseen noise gives guards an approximate area to investigate in exploration, never an exact target. Direct overwatch cone, point at the map, and click to set facing. Gold tiles show visible torso firing areas; dark gold tiles are obstructed or outside identification range. Fire within limits the cone and reaction distance, defaulting to weapon range. Overwatch reserves one normal shot now and fires once during the guard turn only inside that cone and distance. Turning, moving, firing, changing stance or equipment cancels it without refund; unused reservations expire next squad turn.

    The test armoury

    Two equipped weapons swap for free. Equipping a stored weapon into either ready slot costs 3 AP in combat. Each ready slot holds one rifle-sized weapon; both can hold long guns and swap freely. Open Inventory (I) for mercenary tabs, a portrait and stats. The backpack is a 6×3 grid with 18 slots: rifles and assault rifles occupy two adjacent horizontal cells, pistols, ammo stacks and utilities one. Select an item then an empty cell to rearrange. One weapon of each type is currently supported. Loot and adjacent teammate transfers are free. Reloading costs 3 AP and draws only compatible finite reserves. Loaded rounds stay with dropped weapons. The AK-47 and SMG have a three-round burst (6 AP); heavy machine guns cost 8 AP per burst. SMG bullets and each of the six buckshot pellets deal 27 base damage, matching pistol bullets. Hands and the NR-40 require an adjacent tile.

    Height and terrain

    Gun range uses horizontal distance plus 1 tile per level uphill. Firing downhill uses normal range with no penalty or bonus. Clear terrain can be seen up to75tiles away; characters can be detected up to60tiles. These are maximum distances, and walls and floors still block sight. Sight uses each species’ displayed facing cone. Movement turns the character; choose a direction and Turn for free. Show sight cone displays the angular extent; terrain still blocks sight. Squad reports do not let a shooter fire outside their own cone. Shooting uphill applies a 15-point cover penalty; stronger 25-point prop cover takes precedence. Solid floors and walls still block shots. Water cannot be walked through; use bridge decks. Fences stop movement but let you see and fire through. Windows allow rays through a simplified central opening; decorative mullions do not affect shots, while sills and side walls remain solid. Closed-door sprites are fixed barriers in this test; the open doorway is passable.

    Read the factory

    Walls sit between tiles and block crossing, sight and fire; doorways are open edge passages. Crates block walking and provide cover from their side (−25 hit chance). Dark ground is unexplored; dim ground is remembered. Guards appear only while visible. Red rings identify opponents, gold your selected worker, green your other workers.

    On touchscreens, pinch to zoom and move two fingers together to pan. Tap once to preview movement, cutting, treatment or travel; tap the same place again to confirm. Mouse clicks follow the same rule. Right-drag or arrows to pan. Wheel or +/− to zoom. C centers your selected worker. G toggles the grid. Local maps are 240 × 240 tiles, with 100 sectors of 24 × 24 and three walkable levels. Use the level selector to focus a floor; lower layers are dark and upper layers pale. Map clicks affect the focused layer. Use it to inspect each floor, sector coordinates to navigate, and Overview to see the full map. Double-click the overview to inspect a sector. Solid upper floors block shots; stair openings and platform edges can permit cross-level sight. The blue travel marker connects this local map to the overmap. Gather every living member within 2 tiles outside combat to travel. Cleared maps remain walkable. Restart resets all local maps in this run. Select wire cutters in the backpack and equip them in either primary or secondary held slot (3 AP in combat, free while exploring). Stand beside a chain-link fence and use Cut fence to open it for 4 AP in combat, free while exploring. Cutters in the backpack cannot cut. Each worker starts with one medkit. Difficulty is chosen for the next run and applied with Restart. Easy automatically stabilizes downed workers until encounter-end recovery at 5 HP; Standard needs treatment. On total defeat, stabilized mercs are captured and bleeding mercs die. The current prototype retains a captured roster in the run record; the planned mid-to-late-game rescue facility is not yet playable. Restart discards the run. Treat a bleeding teammate from an adjacent tile before their sixth squad-turn end; walls prevent treatment. Medical skill reduces stabilization from 12 to 3 AP. Stabilized teammates recover to 5 HP when the encounter ends. An entirely incapacitated squad loses the encounter; stabilization determines capture versus death.

    Stance and exposed targets

    Standing, kneeling and prone change eye height as well as movement cost. Low cover can hide a prone merc completely. A window or crate may hide legs while leaving the head or torso visible. Aim locations hidden from the selected shooter are disabled, even if another teammate can see them. Torso remains the default: select an exposed head or other region to shoot around cover. Guards can choose exposed regions too. Enemy sprites show the body regions your squad can identify; sight updates immediately when stance changes. The sight-lobe overlay still shows theoretical range rather than an exact outline around obstacles.

    Weapon ranges

    Unarmed and knives reach one tile, including diagonals on the same floor when the corner is clear. Flamethrowers reach 10; pistols and shotguns 12; SMGs 20; AK-47s and rifles 24; heavy machine guns 28; sniper rifles 36. Launchers have a 22-tile effective range and RPGs 40. Accuracy penalties scale with each weapon’s range. Pistols retain poor accuracy; rifles and sniper rifles are more precise at range. Sniper shots cost 8 AP. Shotguns fire six independently colliding pellets for one shell and 5 AP; the pattern spreads with distance and may strike several units, including allies. Shotguns and sniper rifles are available beside Yakov and Anya in the Factory test map.

    Bullets and friendly fire

    Every bullet follows a physical path. A miss scatters and can hit another soldier, including allies beside the shooter or behind the target. Bursts trace each round separately. Even an accurate shot hits the first body or solid obstacle in its path. The firing panel flags units directly in the line of fire; a clear center line does not guarantee safety from scatter. Bullets can continue beyond their intended target, up to 1.5 times the weapon targeting range. Tracers show the actual impacts. Stray torso hits can also detonate loaded flamethrower tanks.

    Merc personalities

    Open a merc’s character sheet to read their background, temperament, relationships and recent memories. Friendly hits cause stress and resentment. Depending on personality, trust and past incidents, a conscious merc may quip or immediately turn and fire one retaliatory shot. This interrupt costs no AP but consumes loaded ammunition. It uses the held weapon, normal range, visibility and bullet scatter; it never reloads or switches weapons for free. A landed reply can provoke another reply, including from a third merc caught in the crossfire. Bursts pause for these exchanges and stop if the shooter falls. Burning or incapacitated mercs cannot retaliate. Stress and fatigue are measured from 0 to 100. Injuries raise both in proportion to HP lost; friendly fire adds another 10 stress. Each enemy kill reduces the killer’s stress by 15. Normal and medical rest remove 5 stress and 10 fatigue per hour; training does not. Fatigue is tracked without an AP or accuracy penalty. Stabilizing a teammate rebuilds trust. Grudges travel with the squad.

    Grenades, launchers and RPGs

    Explosive supplies are beside Anya, Misha and Vera in the Factory test map. Take and equip them through Inventory. Grenades cost 5 AP, reach 10 tiles and carry three ready throws. The grenade launcher costs 6 AP and has a 22-tile effective range; the RPG costs 7 AP and has a 40-tile effective range. Launchers and RPGs load one round at a time. Reload from reserves for 3 AP in combat. Beyond effective range, aim falls to 10% with heavy impact scatter; maximum range is twice effective range. Height advantage adds 3 tiles per level to grenade and launcher range. Throws cannot exceed their range.

    Select an enemy or use Aim at terrain, then click the same tile twice to fire. The outline shows the intended blast radius, not a guarantee of where a scattered round will land. Grenades arc; rockets travel straight. Bodies, walls, windows, cover and ceilings can intercept them. Blasts have a 3-tile radius (4 for RPGs), lose damage with 3D distance, and harm allies and the shooter too. Nearby walls, doors, fences, furniture and trees can be destroyed, opening movement and sight lines. Surviving structures block blast propagation. Structural floors, ground and water remain intact; removing a roof decoration does not collapse its floor. Explosives use area damage rather than body-part aim.

    Flamethrowers and fire

    Flamethrowers reach 10 tiles, cost 6 AP and hold four fuel bursts. Hits deal 180 damage within 3 tiles, falling to 45 at 10 tiles. Close hits kill most units outright; survivors burn and panic. Pick one up beside Yakov in the Factory test map. Loaded tanks can explode when hit: 25% for torso shots, 90% when the held flamethrower is targeted. The wearer and everyone in the eight adjacent tiles die permanently, including allies. A 5-tile radius catches fire on that floor for three turns. Burning survivors cannot act and run randomly for three turns. Walls block attacks and panic movement; the tank fire radius crosses walls. Water does not burn.
